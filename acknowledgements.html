Parish Data

Parish names, addresses, websites, and other basic facts in this directory come from the Episcopal Asset Map, a map of Episcopal Church congregations, schools, and ministries of The Episcopal Church. Some details also come from parish and diocesan websites.

Given the unfortunate fact that the vast majority of Episcopal parishes do not have official doctrine beyond the Book of Common Prayer, let alone publish their stances on the issues people care about, some degree of interpretive liberty has been taken in categorizing and describing social and theological viewpoints held by each parish. In particular, when it comes to the subjects of women's ordination and LGBTQ+ affirmation, descriptions are based on observed practices rather than formal doctrinal statements. This means that if there is, for example, a female deacon serving at a parish, but said parish does not make any public statement on women's ordination to the priesthood or episcopacy, they are by default categorized theologically at the lowest verifiable level of their practice. Meaning, they may not beleive in any restriction on the ordination of women, but since the only publicly available data is that they accept female deacons, I do not attmept to make further inferences. Likewise, if a diocesan bishop is a woman or LGBTQ+ person (as in the case of Eastern North Carolina, where the diocesan bishop is a lesbian), it can generally be assumed that every parish in the diocese accepts both women's ordination and LGBTQ+ ordination to all three orders of ministry, unless there is evidence to the contrary (e.g. DEPO).

Entries marked as "Verified" were confirmed through personal contact with or visits to parishes. See the Terms and Conditions for more on how data from this site may be reused.

Diocesan boundaries are drawn from county outlines in the U.S. Census Bureau’s cartographic boundary files (2017 edition), via us-atlas by Mike Bostock.

The Diocese of Navajoland follows the Navajo Nation Reservation boundary from the Census Bureau’s TIGERweb.

The assignment of counties to dioceses is this site’s own and is approximate; boundaries are not an official publication of the U.S. Census Bureau or The Episcopal Church.
